Battery Configuration

Configure series/parallel battery packs and calculate total capacity, voltage, and discharge limits.

Understanding Battery Configuration Calculations

This calculator helps you configure series (S) and parallel (P) battery pack arrangements for your UAV. Understanding these configurations is essential for achieving the desired voltage, capacity, and discharge capabilities for your application.

Series Configuration (S)

Connecting cells in series adds their voltages while keeping capacity constant:

Series Voltage V_total = V_cell × S

For example, a 6S LiPo pack has 6 cells in series: 6 × 3.7V = 22.2V nominal. Series configuration increases pack voltage to match motor requirements but doesn't affect capacity.

Parallel Configuration (P)

Connecting packs in parallel adds their capacities while keeping voltage constant:

Parallel Capacity C_total = C_cell × P

For example, 2P configuration with 1500mAh cells gives 3000mAh total. Parallel configuration increases flight time but doesn't affect voltage.

Energy Storage

Total energy stored in the pack:

Energy Calculation Energy (Wh) = Capacity (Ah) × Voltage (V)

Energy in watt-hours represents how much work the battery can do. Higher energy means longer flight times or more power available for the same duration.

Discharge Rating (C-rating)

Maximum safe continuous current based on capacity and C-rating:

Max Current I_max = (Capacity / 1000) × C-rating

For example, a 1500mAh 30C battery can deliver 45A continuous (1.5Ah × 30C = 45A). Burst ratings are higher but should only be used briefly to avoid damage.

Internal Resistance

Pack resistance affects voltage sag under load:

Total Resistance R_total = (R_cell × S) / P

Series connections add resistance, while parallel connections reduce it. Lower resistance means better performance under high current draw and less voltage sag.

Energy Density

Energy per unit weight indicates efficiency:

Energy Density Energy Density = Energy (Wh) / Weight (kg)

Typical values: LiPo (~150-170 Wh/kg), Li-Ion (~200-250 Wh/kg), LiFePO4 (~90-110 Wh/kg). Higher energy density means more flight time for the same weight.

Limitations

  • Cell matching: When building parallel packs, cells must be closely matched in voltage, capacity, and internal resistance. Mismatched cells can lead to unbalanced charging and reduced lifespan.
  • C-rating inflation: Manufacturer C-ratings are often overstated. Conservative estimates are recommended, especially for sustained high-current applications.
  • Voltage sag: Calculations use nominal voltages. Under high load, voltage drops due to internal resistance. Actual performance may be lower than calculated values.
  • Temperature effects: Cold temperatures significantly reduce capacity and increase internal resistance. LiPo performance degrades below 0°C.
  • Balancing required: Series configurations require proper cell balancing during charging to prevent damage and ensure longevity. Always use a balance charger.

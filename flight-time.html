Understanding Flight Time Calculations

This calculator estimates flight duration based on battery capacity, power consumption, and operational reserves. Accurate flight time prediction is critical for mission planning and safe operation.

Basic Flight Time Formula

Flight time depends on available energy and average power draw:

Flight Time Calculation Time (minutes) = (Capacity / Current) × 60

where Capacity is in Ah and Current is the average draw in Amps. This gives the theoretical maximum time, which must be adjusted for usable capacity and reserves.

Usable Capacity

Batteries shouldn't be discharged to 0%. Usable capacity based on safe discharge depth:

Usable Capacity C_usable = C_total × (Discharge_depth / 100)

For LiPo batteries, 80% discharge depth (down to 3.3V/cell) balances flight time with battery longevity. Deeper discharges reduce cycle life significantly.

Reserve Margin

A safety reserve ensures you land before critical battery depletion:

Flight Time with Reserve T_flight = T_usable × (1 - Reserve / 100)

Typical reserves are 20-30%. This accounts for unexpected headwinds, navigation errors, or higher-than-expected power consumption. Never plan to use 100% of calculated flight time.

C-Rating During Flight

Current draw relative to battery capacity affects performance:

C-Rate C-rate = Current / Capacity

Higher C-rates cause more voltage sag and reduce effective capacity. Staying below 50% of the battery's rated continuous C-rating maintains better performance and extends battery life.

Voltage Sag Effect

Under load, battery voltage drops due to internal resistance:

Effective Voltage V_eff = V_nom × (1 - sag%/100)

Typical sag is 5-15% depending on load and battery quality. This affects available power and must be accounted for in ESC low-voltage cutoff settings.

Limitations

  • Assumes constant power: Real flights involve varying power consumption (takeoff, climbing, hovering, cruising). Use average values for typical flight profiles.
  • Environmental factors: Wind, temperature, humidity, and altitude significantly affect flight time. Cold weather can reduce capacity by 20-40%. Always test in similar conditions.
  • Battery aging: As batteries age, their capacity and internal resistance degrade. Expect 10-20% reduction after 50-100 cycles. Recalibrate estimates periodically.
  • Weight changes: Payload variations directly affect power consumption. Heavier aircraft require more power to hover and have shorter flight times.
  • Safety margins are critical: Never rely on calculated maximums. Always maintain adequate reserves and land with battery remaining for emergencies.
